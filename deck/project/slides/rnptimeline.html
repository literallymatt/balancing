So when could any of this happen? NESO laid out its plan at the Operational Transparency Forum at the end of October. The deciding part is nearly done. NESO consulted on the five reforms through the Call for Input in February and published the feedback in July. An Industry Expert Panel has been working through the detail since April. The cost-benefit analysis was being finalised in October, and NESO says the results will be published. In December NESO sends its recommendation to DESNZ and Ofgem. But NESO only recommends: the decision sits with Ofgem and the Secretary of State, and there’s no published date for it, so realistically it’s a 2027 decision. If it goes ahead, implementation is phased. Through 2027 and 2028 there’s a consultation on the tolerances, meaning how closely FPNs have to match traded positions. The first go-live is planned for the first quarter of 2029, when new assets above 30 megawatts would have to join the Balancing Mechanism. In 2030 that drops to new assets above 10 megawatts, and in 2032 to all assets above 1 megawatt, applied to existing sites too, with assets below 10 megawatts giving NESO visibility rather than bidding in. Shorter settlement follows the meters: 15-minute settlement for the wholesale market and large sites with Code of Practice metering in 2032, and for homes and small businesses in 2035, once their meters are upgraded. For our customers the message is that nothing changes overnight. But by the early 2030s a multi-megawatt site is likely to be visible to NESO, and possibly in the BM, whether it chooses to be or not. Getting ready early is the opportunity.
