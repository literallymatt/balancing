Here’s what these pressures look like on a bad day. 23 June was the worst evening of the summer heatwave. Gas plants had lost capacity in the heat, demand was running above forecast, and France was hotter and shorter than us, so instead of importing 3.5 gigawatts from France at 7pm we ended up exporting 1.5. The chart shows what that did to Balancing Mechanism payments: almost nothing during the day, then a climb from 4pm to nearly a million pounds per half hour at 20:30, when the system buy price hit £800 a megawatt-hour. NESO issued 4,750 BM instructions in four hours, frequency sat low for most of two hours with a minimum of 49.657 hertz, and it took an emergency instruction to stop exports on BritNed and 500 megawatts of emergency assistance from France to recover. NESO’s review is still preliminary and makes no findings yet, but it’s a vivid picture of how expensive it gets when flexibility runs out at the evening peak, and how valuable it would have been to have more demand that could step down.
