Now the money. This chart shows what NESO spends each year balancing the system. In 2015 it was around half a billion pounds. By 2020 it was £1.3 billion. 2022/23 was the gas crisis spike at £4.1 billion, which was mostly about the price of energy rather than how much balancing was needed. What I want you to notice is what happened next: wholesale prices fell, but balancing costs didn’t go back to where they were. They have climbed again, to £2.7 billion in 2024/25 and roughly £3.1 billion last year, and the driver is constraints. NESO’s own projections show costs peaking at around £8 billion in 2030 as new wind connects faster than the network is reinforced, and NESO says around £4 billion of that could be avoided if critical network projects are delivered earlier. One note on the data: 2015 and 2020 are calendar years from Elexon; the rest are NESO financial years. And the 2025/26 figure is an aggregation of NESO’s monthly reports, because the annual report wasn’t out when this was written.
