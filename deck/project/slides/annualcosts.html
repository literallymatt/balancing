Actuals: our analysis of NESO Daily BSUoS Cost Data by calendar year (2026 to 29 Oct). Forecasts: NESO BSUoS forecast (Oct 2026) for Nov 2026 to Dec 2027; NESO 2025 Annual Balancing Costs Report for 2030. Bases differ, so treat forecasts as indicative.

Now the money. This chart shows what NESO spent balancing the system each calendar year, from its own daily cost data. In 2018 it was about £640 million. By 2020 it was £1.3 billion. 2022 was the gas crisis spike at £3 billion, driven by high prices for reserve and other services rather than by how much balancing was needed. What I want you to notice is the blue part of each bar: constraint costs, the money NESO pays because the network can’t carry power from where it’s generated to where it’s used. Constraints went from about half a billion in 2018 to £2.1 billion last year, 90% of the total. And this year, with two months still to go, NESO has already spent £2.9 billion, more than double 2020 and within touching distance of the 2022 gas-crisis peak, and this time nearly all of it is constraints. Looking ahead, the orange bars are NESO’s own forecasts, not our data. Add NESO’s forecast for November and December and 2026 lands at about £3.7 billion. Its October BSUoS forecast puts 2027 at about £4.2 billion. NESO hasn’t published annual figures for 2028 and 2029, but its 2025 report projects total balancing costs could peak at around £8 billion by 2030 as new wind connects faster than the network is reinforced, and says around £4 billion of that could be avoided if critical network projects are delivered earlier. One note on the data: this daily series covers fewer costs than NESO’s annual balancing cost reports, so it sits below the headline figures you may have seen, for example £2.1 billion here against £2.7 billion reported for 2024/25. We use it because it’s the only NESO series we can cut into calendar years.
