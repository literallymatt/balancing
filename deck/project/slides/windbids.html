Here is the constraint story in our own data. This chart shows only bids, the money NESO pays units to reduce output or increase demand, broken down by technology. Since January 2023 NESO has paid out about £1.5 billion in bids, and 94% of it went to wind farms, overwhelmingly in Scotland, being paid to stop because the network can’t carry their output south. NESO’s own daily cost data says constraints made up 90% of its balancing costs in 2025, £2.1 billion, and this year it has already spent £2.7 billion on constraints by the end of October. And look at the orange number: supplier and virtual lead party units, which is where aggregated demand side response sits, received about 0.3% of those bid payments, under £5 million. In other words, when NESO needs someone to soak up surplus power behind a constraint, it almost always pays a wind farm to switch off rather than paying demand to switch on. That’s a huge gap, and it’s the opportunity we’ll come back to.
