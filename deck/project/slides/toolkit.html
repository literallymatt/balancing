02 · NESO’s role

How NESO balances: energy and system

Energy balancing

Matching supply and demand

  • Purpose: keep total supply equal to demand every second, so frequency holds near 50 Hz
  • Its own markets: reserve (Quick, Slow and Balancing Reserve) and frequency response (Dynamic Containment, Moderation, Regulation)

System balancing

Managing grid conditions

  • Purpose: keep power flows within network limits, and voltage and stability in range, in the right places
  • Its own markets: Local Constraint Market, constraint intertrips, and stability and voltage contracts

Used for both: NESO picks for quantity, or for location

Balancing Mechanism

Bids and offers after Gate Closure, and where the cost growth shows

Trades

Wholesale trades before Gate Closure, and interconnector trades

Demand Flexibility Service

National turn-down for margin, and zonal turn-up or down for constraints

Source: NESO heat review (Sept 2026), section 5.7 and Appendix C (order of actions); NESO balancing services and trading pages

NESO’s actions fall into two kinds, and NESO labels them this way itself. Energy balancing is about quantity: making total supply equal total demand every second, so frequency stays near 50 hertz. System balancing is about where the power is: keeping flows on each line within its limit, and keeping voltage and stability right in each area. Each has a few markets of its own. For energy, NESO buys reserve ahead of time, Quick, Slow and Balancing Reserve, and frequency response services that react within a second or so to sudden swings. For system, it has the Local Constraint Market, which pays flexible sites behind the Scotland to England boundary to turn up or down, intertrips that disconnect generation automatically after a fault, and longer-term stability and voltage contracts. But the biggest tools are shared, and the same action can be taken for either reason. The Balancing Mechanism is the main one: an offer pays a unit to increase output or reduce demand, a bid pays it to reduce output or increase demand. NESO also trades: in the wholesale market before Gate Closure, and across interconnectors, mostly with the neighbouring system operators. The next slide covers interconnectors. And the Demand Flexibility Service now runs nationally for margin and zonally for constraints. When a constraint binds, NESO bids wind down behind it and offers gas up in front of it, and that system balancing is where the cost growth is. Keep this split in mind: flexibility has proven itself on the energy side, and the newer, bigger prize is on the system side.
