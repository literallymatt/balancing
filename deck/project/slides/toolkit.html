02 · NESO’s role

NESO balances for two reasons: energy and system

Energy balancing

Matching supply and demand

  • Purpose: keep total supply equal to demand every second, so frequency holds near 50 Hz
  • Markets: wholesale and interconnector trades, BM bids and offers, reserve and frequency response, and the Demand Flexibility Service

System balancing

Managing grid conditions

  • Purpose: keep power flows within network limits, and voltage and stability in range, in the right places
  • Markets: BM bids and offers chosen by location, constraint trades, the Local Constraint Market, DFS constraint actions, and stability and voltage contracts

The BM, trades and DFS appear on both sides: NESO uses them for quantity or for location

Source: NESO heat review (Sept 2026), section 5.7 and Appendix C; NESO balancing services pages

Before we look at when NESO acts, it helps to know why. NESO’s actions fall into two kinds, and NESO labels them this way itself. Energy balancing is about quantity: making total supply equal total demand every second, so frequency stays near 50 hertz. To do that it trades in the wholesale market and across interconnectors, accepts bids and offers in the Balancing Mechanism, holds reserve such as Quick, Slow and Balancing Reserve, buys frequency response that reacts within a second or so, and calls the Demand Flexibility Service. System balancing is about where the power is: keeping flows on each line within its limit, and keeping voltage and stability right in each area. Here the same BM bids and offers are chosen for their location, alongside constraint trades, the Local Constraint Market for flexible sites behind the Scotland to England boundary, DFS constraint actions, and longer-term stability and voltage contracts. You’ll notice some tools appear on both sides: the BM, trades and DFS can be used for either job. The next slide shows when in the timeline NESO uses each of them. Keep this split in mind: flexibility has proven itself on the energy side, and the newer, bigger prize is on the system side.
