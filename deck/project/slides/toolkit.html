02 · NESO’s role

Two kinds of balancing, one main tool

Energy balancing

Matching supply and demand

  • Purpose: keep total supply equal to demand every second, so frequency holds near 50 Hz
  • Its own markets: reserve (Quick, Slow and Balancing Reserve) and frequency response (Dynamic Containment, Moderation, Regulation)

System balancing

Managing grid conditions

  • Purpose: keep power flows within network limits, and voltage and stability in range, in the right places
  • Its own markets: Local Constraint Market, and stability and voltage contracts

The main tool for both

NESO’s actions fall into two kinds, and NESO labels them this way itself. Energy balancing is about quantity: making total supply equal total demand every second, so frequency stays near 50 hertz. System balancing is about where the power is: keeping flows on each line within its limit, and keeping voltage and stability right in each area. Each has a few tools of its own. For energy, NESO buys reserve ahead of time, Quick, Slow and Balancing Reserve, and frequency response services that react within a second or so to sudden swings. For system, it has the Local Constraint Market, which pays flexible sites behind the Scotland to England boundary to turn up or down, and longer-term stability and voltage contracts. But the main tool is shared, and that is the Balancing Mechanism. The same bid or offer can be accepted for either reason, for quantity or for location, and the BM is where most of the money goes: £3.15 billion paid out in the first ten months of this year. When a constraint binds, NESO bids wind down behind it and offers gas up in front of it, so that is where the cost growth is. Before Gate Closure NESO can also trade in the wholesale market and across interconnectors, and call the Demand Flexibility Service, again for either reason.
