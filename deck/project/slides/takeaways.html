To wrap up, five things. One: costs are up. NESO spent about £3.4 billion balancing the grid by the end of October, nearly double 2020, and on its own forecast the year lands at about £4.3 billion. Two: constraints are most of it, about 70% of last year’s cost and over three quarters of this year’s so far. That’s money paid because the network can’t carry power from where it’s made to where it’s used. Three: the market doesn’t see the grid. Up to Gate Closure everyone trades on one national price as if the wires were unlimited, and NESO then pays twice in the Balancing Mechanism to undo what the network can’t deliver. Four: the reforms help, but they won’t remove congestion. Government ruled out zonal pricing, and NESO’s balancing reforms, 15-minute settlement, lower BM thresholds and a firmer Gate Closure, start from 2029 if approved. By NESO’s own account they make constraints cheaper to manage, not go away. Five: that’s the opening for our industrial and commercial customers. NESO will keep paying visible, controllable sites in the right place to move, today through DFS and the BM, and from 2028 through new constraint markets, and the reforms open more routes to get paid. Happy to take questions.
