To wrap up, four things. One: balancing costs are exploding, with £3.15 billion of Balancing Mechanism payments so far this year, already more than the whole of 2025, and a sharp jump this autumn. Two: the causes are structural, not one-offs: network constraints, a single national price that ignores the network, and a growing fleet of assets NESO can’t see or use. Three: government ruled out zonal pricing, so the fixes run through reformed national pricing; NESO’s reforms will improve visibility and price signals, but by its own account they won’t remove the congestion. Four: flexible demand from our industrial and commercial customers addresses every one of these problems, and the routes for them to be paid for it, DFS, the BM, capacity and reserve markets and avoided costs, are opening up. Happy to take questions.
