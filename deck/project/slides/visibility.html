The second structural problem is visibility. The system has moved from a few hundred big power stations to a huge number of smaller assets connected to distribution networks: solar, batteries, small gas, CHP and flexible demand. NESO estimates about 33 gigawatts, more than three quarters of distributed assets, are neither visible to it nor available for real-time balancing, and that at sunny, windy times more than half of supply could come from units outside the Balancing Mechanism. Then there’s behaviour. Parties can submit FPNs that don’t match what they’ve traded, and some deliberately sit long or short to profit from the imbalance price, known as NIV chasing. NESO estimates around 2 gigawatts of this on one day last November caused frequency oscillations. And the 30-minute settlement period means big step changes at every half-hour boundary, which NESO has to cover with fast frequency services; that spend roughly tripled to almost £9 million a month by last October. On top of that, most flexibility is still procured nationally, so an asset in the right place for the network isn’t rewarded for being there.
