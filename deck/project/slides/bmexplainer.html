02 · NESO’s role

How the Balancing Mechanism works

1

Units post prices

With its FPN, each BM unit sets prices per MWh to move up or down

2

NESO accepts in real time

It picks the cheapest actions that also work for the network, so location can beat price

3

Paid as bid, recovered on bills

Each unit gets its own price; the cost flows to suppliers through BSUoS

Offer: more power

Generate more or use less. NESO pays the unit’s offer price

Bid: less power

Generate less or use more. Gas pays NESO back for fuel saved; wind bids negative, so NESO pays it

Who takes part

Generators, batteries, large demand, and smaller sites grouped by Virtual Lead Parties: the route in for I&C flexibility

Source: Elexon BSC and BM guidance; NESO heat review (Sept 2026), section 5.7; NESO BSUoS

Because the BM is where most of the money goes, it’s worth a minute on how it works. At Gate Closure, along with its FPN, every unit in the mechanism submits prices: an offer price per megawatt-hour to move up from its plan, and a bid price to move down. Then, in real time, NESO’s control room accepts the actions it needs. It looks for the cheapest, but they have to work for the network, so a cheaper unit in the wrong place loses to a dearer one in the right place. That’s why constraints are so expensive. Each accepted action is paid at the unit’s own price, pay-as-bid, not a single clearing price, and Elexon settles it. NESO recovers all of it from suppliers through BSUoS, Balancing Services Use of System charges, which since 2023 fall entirely on demand, so our customers pay it. An offer is more power on the system: a generator turning up, or a demand site turning down. A bid is less power: a generator turning down or demand turning up. Most bids are cheap or even pay NESO back, because a gas plant that turns down saves fuel. Wind is the exception: it bids negative prices because it loses output and subsidy, so NESO pays it to stop. Who takes part? Generators, batteries, large demand sites, and, since the BM was opened up, smaller sites aggregated by Virtual Lead Parties. That last group is how I&C flexibility gets in, which we’ll come back to.
