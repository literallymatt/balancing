NESO’s own verdict: these reforms cut the cost of managing redispatch, but don’t remove the congestion that causes it.

Sources: NESO RNP Balancing, Settlement and Dispatch Call for Input (Feb 2026) and summary of feedback and initial views (July 2026)

Here are the balancing reforms NESO consulted on, mapped to the problems we’ve just seen, with where each stood after NESO summarised 81 responses in July. Shorter settlement, moving from 30 to 15 minutes, had the most support and is now the base case, starting with wholesale and large users. Lowering the BM participation threshold so NESO can see and dispatch more assets is also supported: the base case is full participation above 10 megawatts and visibility for assets from 1 megawatt. Aligning the market trading deadline with Gate Closure, and requiring FPNs to match traded positions, both target the open positions and NIV chasing we discussed, but industry pushed back, so they’ll be assessed as an add-on package, with FPN matching at portfolio level. Unit bidding, tying bids to specific units rather than portfolios, was unpopular and won’t get a recommendation until the end of 2027, alongside wider dispatch reform. Decisions on the first package are due after cost-benefit analysis later this year, with implementation from 2027. The key line is at the bottom: NESO itself says these reforms make redispatch cheaper to manage but don’t remove the congestion. Something still has to absorb surplus power in the north and fill gaps in the south. That’s where flexible demand comes in.
