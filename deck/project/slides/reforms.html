NESO’s own verdict: these reforms cut the cost of managing redispatch, but don’t remove the congestion that causes it.

Sources: NESO, RNP Call for Input (Feb 2026) and Summary of Feedback (Jul 2026); NESO, Operational Transparency Forum (28 Oct 2026)

Here are the balancing reforms NESO is working on, mapped to the problems we’ve just seen, as they stood at NESO’s Operational Transparency Forum at the end of October. First, shorter settlement, moving from 30 minutes to a 15-minute base case, with 5 minutes tested as a sensitivity. Second, a lower BM participation threshold, so NESO can see and dispatch more assets: full participation for existing sites from 30 megawatts and new ones from 10, and visibility of new generation, including batteries, from 1 megawatt. Then two that industry pushed back on, so they’re less certain. Moving the market trading deadline back to Gate Closure, which would undo the P342 change we mentioned earlier, and requiring FPNs to match traded positions at portfolio level, with tolerances still to be consulted on. Both target the open positions and NIV chasing we discussed. Unit-level bidding is still being assessed, and NESO says its dispatch reform options will come later. An Industry Expert Panel has been meeting since April to work through the detail. The key line is at the bottom: NESO itself says these reforms make redispatch cheaper to manage but don’t remove the congestion. Something still has to absorb surplus power in the north and fill gaps in the south. That’s where flexible demand comes in, and the next slide shows when all this could happen.
