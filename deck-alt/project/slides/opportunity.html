Sources: NESO, Demand Flexibility Service announcement (25 Mar 2026) and service page; DESNZ, RNP Delivery Plan (Apr 2026)

So what’s the opportunity for our customers in practice? There are four routes, and they stack. First, the Demand Flexibility Service, which was redesigned this year: the minimum size fell from 1 megawatt to 0.1, customers can now be paid to turn up as well as down, procurement is zonal, and from October it buys constraint actions too. Second, the Balancing Mechanism itself: through an aggregator or virtual lead party, flexible demand can earn the same bid and offer prices that generators earn, and we’ve seen how high those have gone. Third, availability payments: the Capacity Market for demand that can reduce at peak, and NESO’s reserve and frequency response markets for sites, often with batteries, that can respond quickly. Fourth, avoided costs: not consuming at peak cuts wholesale, imbalance and red-band network charges, and 15-minute settlement will sharpen that. And coming down the line, the government’s trial of removing final consumption levies for demand in constrained areas could mean very cheap power for sites in the right places when wind is surplus.
