Actuals: NESO Monthly Balancing Services Summary by calendar year (2018 to Mar 2019 rebuilt from NESO daily data; Sep–Oct 2026 from NESO early data plus a small estimate). Forecasts (indicative): NESO BSUoS Forecast, Oct 2026; NESO Annual Balancing Costs Report 2025.

Now the money. This chart shows what NESO spent balancing the system each calendar year, on the same basis as the headline figures NESO publishes. In 2018 it was about £1.1 billion. By 2020 it was £1.8 billion. 2022 was the gas crisis spike at £4.2 billion, driven by high prices for reserve and other services rather than by how much balancing was needed. What I want you to notice is the blue part of each bar: constraint costs, the money NESO pays because the network can’t carry power from where it’s generated to where it’s used. Constraints went from about £0.6 billion in 2018 to £2.1 billion last year, 70% of the total, and that share is still climbing. And this year, with two months still to go, NESO has already spent about £3.4 billion, nearly double 2020’s £1.8 billion, and more than three quarters of it is constraints. September and October alone cost over £1.1 billion. Looking ahead, the orange bars are NESO’s own published forecasts. Add NESO’s forecast for November and December and 2026 lands at about £4.3 billion, which would beat the 2022 gas-crisis record. Its October BSUoS forecast puts 2027 at about £4.2 billion. NESO hasn’t published annual figures for 2028 and 2029, but its 2025 report projects total balancing costs could peak at around £8 billion by 2030 as new wind connects faster than the network is reinforced, and says around £4 billion of that could be avoided if critical network projects are delivered earlier. One note on the data: NESO’s monthly summary runs to August, so September and October are its early figures plus a small estimate, and 2026 may move a little when the monthly summaries catch up.
