So what’s being done? The obvious fix for the locational problem would have been zonal pricing: splitting GB into price zones so power in constrained areas gets cheaper and the market sees the network. That was a central option in the government’s Review of Electricity Market Arrangements, REMA, which began in 2022. In July 2025 the government ruled zonal pricing out and kept a single national price, citing the risks to investment and delivery. Instead it is pursuing what it calls Reformed National Pricing, with a delivery plan published in April this year. It has three pillars. First, get new assets built in the right places, through a Strategic Spatial Energy Plan, connections reform and locational network charges. Second, cut constraint costs, mainly by building network faster, and notably through a large trial removing final consumption levies for demand in constrained parts of Scotland and the East of England, so users there can get cheap or free power when wind is surplus. Third, reform balancing itself, which NESO is leading. Government says ‘no change’ is not an option. Its plan had decisions starting from the second half of this year; in practice NESO recommends in December and the decision falls in 2027.
