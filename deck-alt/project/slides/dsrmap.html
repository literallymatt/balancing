Here’s how flexible demand from our customers lines up against each difficulty. Soaring Balancing Mechanism costs: the average price NESO paid for accepted offers doubled between October last year and October this year, from about £121 to £245 a megawatt-hour in our analysis of Elexon data, so every megawatt of demand that can step down at the peak displaces some very expensive generation. Constraints: demand can turn up behind a constraint, using surplus wind that would otherwise be curtailed, or turn down in front of it. Today wind farms get 94% of the turn-down money and demand gets 0.3%, so there is enormous room for demand to compete. No locational price: while the wholesale price stays national, locational signals are appearing elsewhere. The Demand Flexibility Service now procures by zone and buys constraint actions, and government is trialling removing levies for demand in constrained areas. Invisible assets: a metered, controllable customer site is exactly the kind of visible, dispatchable resource NESO says it lacks. And coarse settlement: when settlement moves to 15 minutes, fast and accurate response becomes more valuable, which suits automated controls on industrial sites.
