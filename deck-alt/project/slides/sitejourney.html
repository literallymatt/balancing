So how does it actually work for the customer? Step one, sign up: the site joins a portfolio run by an aggregator registered as a Virtual Lead Party, which is how sites below the big-generator scale get into the BM, or signs up for the Demand Flexibility Service, which now buys constraint actions too. Metering and controls are tested. Step two, declare: for the BM, by Gate Closure the aggregator submits the site’s plan, its FPN, and the prices at which it’s willing to move up or down. Step three, the important one for constraints: NESO picks the site because of where it is. In the BM that happens in real time; DFS buys day-ahead or within the day. Step four, deliver: the site’s controls ramp the CHP or dispatch the battery, typically within minutes, and the meters record what happened. Step five, get paid: the volume is settled against the site’s plan or baseline and the aggregator passes on the customer’s share. What the customer needs is plant that can move on instruction, half-hourly metering, automatic control, and ideally a site in a constrained area. Where we come in is finding those sites in our portfolio, connecting them to [our VLP partner, or our own VLP], and making sure their supply contract and imbalance position stay whole when they respond.
